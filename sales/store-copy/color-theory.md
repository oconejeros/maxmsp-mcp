## Title

**Color Theory — Music Theory Visualizer for Ableton Live**

## Hook

Eight views of what you're playing, at once: the generalized Tonnetz lattice, the chromatic
and fifths circles, the voice-leading space, piano, guitar, the diatonic Tonnetz and
Gollin's 3-D Tonnetz. Plus a study mode that walks all 351 set classes. Every note is
color-coded by pitch class — the wheel rotates to whichever key you want at the center.

## What's included

- **Generalized Tonnetz**: pick the interval-class vector of the triangle edges (3 4 5 =
  the classic one) and the lattice re-forms. Triangles fill in when their three notes
  sound; harmonic neighbours; P / L / R transforms.
- **Chromatic and fifths circles** with configurable connections (polygon, every dyad, or
  by interval class).
- **Voice-leading space** (the 12 trichords).
- **Piano** (one octave or full MIDI range) and **guitar** (tunings, diatonic scale mode,
  4-fret chord fingering).
- **Diatonic Tonnetz**: just the 7 notes of the key, with **key auto-detection**
  (Krumhansl-Schmuckler over a window of recent chords).
- **Gollin's 3-D Tonnetz**: 4-note chords (7ths) as tetrahedra in isometric projection.
- **Study mode**: dial a Forte class + rotation + tonic; the set shows on every panel with
  MIDI frozen out. Sort the walk by McKay dissonance, filter by symmetry.
- **Rotatable pitch-class palette** (hue given to C; the whole wheel turns).
- Recent-note trace, region path, transpose / invert preview without touching the MIDI.

## Requirements

- Ableton Live 12 or later with **Max for Live**.
- MIDI-effect device on the track you want to analyse.

## Theory background

Generalized Tonnetz and harmonic-complex analysis (Bigo et al., *Computer Music Journal*
2015); McKay dissonance; Krumhansl-Schmuckler key finding.
